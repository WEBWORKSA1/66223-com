Why 66223 suits remote workers

66223 is a well-educated, established suburban ZIP. About 67% of adults 25 and older hold a bachelor's degree or higher, and the median household income is $113,707. Many homes have the extra bedroom or finished basement that makes a real office possible. It's also in the Central time zone, which is convenient for working with both coasts: you're two hours ahead of the West Coast and one hour behind the East.

The trade-off is that the area is car-dependent, with a Walk Score of 33. Remote work here can get isolating if you don't plan for it. The sections below cover the physical setup, the connection and the habits that make it sustainable.

Equipment checklist

  • An adjustable chair and a desk at the right height (or a sit-stand desk).
  • An external monitor at eye level, plus a separate keyboard and mouse.
  • A decent webcam and a headset or microphone. Audio quality matters more than video.
  • Soft furnishings such as a rug, curtains or bookshelves to cut echo.
  • A desk lamp or soft light facing you for video calls.
Power tip: spring and summer storms are a real factor in Kansas and can cause outages. A small UPS on your modem and router can keep you online through brief flickers, and a phone hotspot plan works as a backup for longer outages.

Internet: questions to ask before you commit

Internet options can differ from one street to the next, even within the same ZIP. Don't assume that the service a neighbor has, or a provider's general coverage map, applies to your address. Verify before you sign a lease or remove contingencies on a purchase.

Questions to ask every provider

  1. What services are available at this exact address, and what type of connection is it (fiber, cable, fixed wireless, other)?
  2. What are the advertised download and upload speeds? Upload speed matters for video calls and sending large files.
  3. Is there a data cap, and what happens if I go over it?
  4. What's the regular price after any promotional period ends, and how long is the promo?
  5. Is there a contract, and what's the early termination fee?
  6. Are there equipment rental fees, and can I use my own modem or router?
  7. What are installation fees and the typical lead time for installation?
  8. Is there a business-class option, and what support or uptime commitments come with it?

Before you move in

  • Ask the seller, landlord or neighbors which providers they've used and how reliable service has been.
  • If you're house hunting, run a speed test during a showing on your phone and see where the existing equipment is installed.
  • Schedule installation before move-in day so you're not working from your car.

Getting out of the house: coworking, libraries and third places

Even dedicated remote workers benefit from a change of scene once or twice a week. Common options in the Kansas City suburbs include:

  • Coworking spaces. Most offer day passes, part-time memberships and dedicated desks. Ask about meeting-room hours, guest policies, parking, access hours and whether there's a quiet zone for calls.
  • Public libraries. Libraries typically offer free Wi-Fi, quiet seating and sometimes bookable study rooms. Check your local library's rules for reserving rooms and taking calls.
  • Your employer's office. If you're hybrid, plan in-office days around team meetings so the commute is worth it.

When comparing coworking memberships, add up a realistic month: membership, parking, gas and the coffee you'll buy. Compare that with the cost of upgrading your home setup.

Staying social and avoiding isolation

  • Schedule it. Put a weekly lunch, workout class or coworking day on your calendar like a meeting.
  • Use the parks. Overland Park has more than 80 city parks, and a midday walk on a trail is the easiest reset there is. The Overland Park Arboretum & Botanical Gardens is a good spot for a longer break.
  • Join something local. Rec leagues, volunteer groups, faith communities, neighborhood associations and HOA events are where suburban friendships often start.
  • Hit the weekend market. The Overland Park Farmers' Market is an easy weekly routine to look forward to.
  • Set a hard stop. When the office is down the hall, work can creep into evenings. A shutdown routine (close the laptop, close the door) helps.

Taxes and remote work: be careful

Consult a tax professional: home office deductions have strict rules that depend on whether you're an employee, self-employed or both, and on how exclusively and regularly you use the space. Rules change, and an incorrect claim can lead to penalties. Get advice for your specific situation.

A few general points worth raising with your tax professional:

  • Whether you qualify for any home office deduction at all, given your employment type.
  • How state income tax works if your employer is based in another state, particularly Missouri, given how many people in the metro cross the state line for work.
  • What records to keep: square footage of the office, utility bills, equipment receipts and any reimbursements from your employer.

For Kansas tax information, start at the Kansas Department of Revenue. If you're self-employed and running a business from home, see our guide to starting a small business in Overland Park and check zoning and home-business rules with the city.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find out what internet is available at a specific address?

Contact providers directly with the full street address and ask what's available there, including connection type and upload speeds. Ask current residents or neighbors too, and don't rely only on general coverage maps.

What internet speed do I need for remote work?

It depends on your work and household. Video calls, large file uploads and several people online at once all need more capacity, especially upload speed. Check your employer's IT recommendations and size your plan for your busiest hour, not the average.

Can I deduct my home office on my taxes?

Maybe, but the rules are strict and depend on your employment status and how you use the space. Talk to a qualified tax professional before claiming any deduction.

Is 66223 walkable for remote workers who want to get out?

Not especially. Its Walk Score is 33, which rates it car-dependent. Most coworking, coffee and errands will involve a short drive, though parks and neighborhood trails offer walkable breaks.
