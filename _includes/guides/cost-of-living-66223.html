The big picture

ZIP 66223 is a high-income suburb. The median household income is $113,707, about 67% of adults 25 and older hold at least a bachelor's degree, and unemployment runs around 3.3%. Those numbers explain the prices: housing demand is strong, and home values climbed from a median of $263,300 in 2011 to $437,500 in 2024.

The overall cost of living, as estimated by BestPlaces, sits about 6.2% above the national average. That is a modest premium on paper, but a single index hides the details. Housing can feel much more expensive than average, while other categories may feel close to normal. The only way to know what it means for you is to build your own budget.

Housing

Housing will be your largest expense whether you rent or buy.

  • Renting: The median gross rent in 66223 is $1,718 a month, and BestPlaces puts a typical 2-bedroom at about $1,790. About 44% of homes here are renter-occupied, so there is a real rental market to shop.
  • Buying: The 2024 median home value was $437,500, and BestPlaces lists a median home price of about $440,700. Your monthly cost includes principal, interest, property taxes, homeowner's insurance and, very often, HOA dues.

A widely used rule of thumb is to keep total housing costs at or below about 28 to 30% of gross income. Use the home affordability calculator to see what price range that implies for you, and the rent vs. buy calculator if you are deciding between the two.

Taxes

Property tax

Kansas assesses homes at 11.5% of appraised market value, and the tax equals the assessed value times the total mill levy divided by 1,000. Overland Park's city levy of about 14.5 mills for 2026 is the lowest of any Johnson County city, and the city's share is only about 13% of a typical bill. The rest goes to the county, schools, the state and other districts. Our Johnson County property tax guide walks through a full worked example, or try the property tax estimator.

Sales tax

The combined sales tax rate in Overland Park is 9.35%. That applies to a wide range of everyday purchases, so build it into your spending estimates rather than treating price tags as final.

Income tax

Kansas has a state income tax. Rates and brackets change, so check the Kansas Department of Revenue for current figures, and talk to a tax professional if you are moving from a state with no income tax or a very different structure.

Utilities

Most homes in the area deal with four separate utility providers: Evergy (electric), WaterOne (water), Kansas Gas Service (natural gas) and Johnson County Wastewater (sewer). Trash is usually handled by a private hauler or arranged through your HOA. Internet and phone come on top of that.

Your utility bills depend heavily on home size, age, insulation, appliance type and how you heat and cool. Kansas summers mean real air-conditioning demand, and winters bring heating costs. The best move: ask the landlord or seller for 12 months of utility history for the specific property. That beats any average.

Transportation

With a Walk Score of 33, 66223 is car-dependent. Plan on a vehicle for most errands and commutes. Your transportation line should include loan or lease payments, insurance, fuel or charging, maintenance, tires, registration and parking where applicable. If your household needs a second or third car, budget for each one separately.

Childcare

With an average household size of about 2.53 people and a family-oriented reputation, childcare is a major cost for many 66223 households. Prices vary widely by age of child, type of care (center, in-home or nanny) and schedule. Call several providers, ask for current rates in writing, and ask about waitlists, registration fees and annual increases. Get on lists early, as popular providers can fill up well in advance.

How to compare 66223 with your current city

  1. Start with your real spending. Pull three months of bank and card statements and sort them into the categories above.
  2. Swap in local housing. Replace your current rent or mortgage with real listings in 66223, including property taxes, insurance and HOA dues.
  3. Adjust taxes. Compare state income tax, sales tax (9.35% here) and property tax side by side.
  4. Rethink transportation. If you walk or use transit now, add the full cost of car ownership.
  5. Check the salary side. A move only makes sense if your take-home pay minus your new costs leaves you better off or meets another goal you care about.
