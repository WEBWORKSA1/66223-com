Key takeaways:
  • Get a full lender pre-approval before you tour seriously; it sets your real budget and makes your offer credible.
  • In Kansas, add radon testing and a close look at the foundation and roof to every inspection plan.
  • Read HOA documents before your review deadline, not after. Dues, rules and reserves vary a lot between subdivisions.
  • Plan on roughly 30 to 60 days from accepted offer to closing as a typical range for a financed purchase.

Know the market you are buying into

ZIP 66223 is a mostly owner-occupied part of south Overland Park, with about 56% of homes owner-occupied and 44% rented. The median home value was $437,500 in 2024, up from $263,300 in 2011, so values have climbed steadily over the last decade-plus. Public schools are served by Blue Valley USD 229, which drives a lot of buyer demand. The area is car-dependent (Redfin Walk Score of 33), so commute and errand distance matter more here than in a walkable core.

For a quick snapshot of incomes, home values and rents, see the 66223 data page. If you are new to the area, the moving to Overland Park guide covers neighborhoods and logistics.

Step 1: Set your budget and get pre-approved

A pre-qualification is a quick estimate. A pre-approval means a lender has pulled your credit and reviewed income, assets and debts. Sellers and listing agents take the second one seriously; the first one, much less so.

  • Gather documents: two recent pay stubs, two years of W-2s or tax returns (more if self-employed), two months of bank statements, and ID.
  • Compare at least three lenders on the same day so rates and fees are comparable. Our mortgage basics guide explains loan types, points and escrow.
  • Budget for the full monthly payment, not just principal and interest. Property taxes and insurance are a meaningful share of the payment in Johnson County. Run numbers with the home affordability calculator and the property tax estimator.
  • Hold cash back for closing costs, moving, and the first repairs. A common rule of thumb is to keep a reserve after closing equal to a few months of housing costs.

Step 2: Choose a buyer's agent

Interview two or three agents. You want someone who actively works south Johnson County and can explain the differences between subdivisions, HOA structures and school boundaries without guessing.

Questions to ask

  • How many buyers have you represented in 66223 and neighboring ZIPs (66085, 66209, 66213, 66221, 66224) in the last year?
  • How are you paid, and what does the buyer agreement commit me to? Get the compensation terms and the agreement length in writing.
  • How do you handle multiple-offer situations, and what terms have actually won recently?
  • Which inspectors, lenders and title companies do you work with, and am I free to choose my own?

Always confirm school assignment for a specific address directly with Blue Valley USD 229. Boundaries can change, and listing remarks are not a guarantee.

Step 3: Tour with a checklist

Keep notes on every home the same way so you can compare later. Look past staging and focus on what is expensive to change: location, lot, layout, roof, foundation, windows, HVAC age and drainage. Check the drive to work and school at the hours you would actually travel.

Step 4: Make a strong, protected offer

Price is only one part of an offer. Sellers also weigh certainty and timing. Work with your agent on:

  • Price and earnest money: a larger deposit signals commitment. Know exactly when it becomes non-refundable.
  • Contingencies: inspection, financing and appraisal contingencies protect you. Shortening a deadline is often a safer way to compete than waiving a contingency outright.
  • Closing date and possession: matching the seller's preferred timing can beat a slightly higher offer.
  • Appraisal gap: if you offer over list, decide in advance how much of any shortfall you could cover in cash.

Step 5: Inspections that matter in Kansas

Hire a general home inspector and add specialists when the general report flags something. Three areas deserve extra attention here.

Radon

Radon is a naturally occurring gas that can collect in basements and lower levels. It is invisible and has no smell, so the only way to know is to test. Ask for a radon test as part of your inspection. If levels come back elevated, a mitigation system is a common and usually manageable fix; negotiate it as a repair or credit.

Foundation and drainage

Soil that swells when wet and shrinks when dry can stress a foundation over time, and poor drainage makes it worse. Look for stair-step cracks in block walls, horizontal cracks, bowing walls, sticking doors, and signs of water in the basement. Check that gutters and downspouts carry water away and that the yard slopes away from the house. If anything looks structural, bring in a structural engineer before your inspection deadline.

Roof

Spring and early-summer storms, including hail, are a real factor in Kansas. Ask the seller for the roof's age and any insurance claim history, and have the inspector look for hail damage, granule loss and soft spots. Roof age can also affect your insurance quote, so get that quote early. The hail and roof insurance guide goes deeper.

Watch out: Inspection deadlines in a contract are firm. Schedule inspectors the day your offer is accepted so specialist follow-ups still fit inside the window.

Step 6: Review the HOA

Many 66223 subdivisions have homeowners associations. Request and read the covenants, bylaws, current budget, recent meeting minutes and the dues schedule. Look for:

  • Monthly or annual dues and what they cover (common areas, pool, trash, snow removal).
  • Reserve fund health and any planned special assessments.
  • Rules on fences, sheds, exterior colors, rentals, home businesses and parking.

Step 7: Appraisal, final loan approval and closing

Your lender orders the appraisal and works toward final approval. Avoid new credit, large unexplained deposits or job changes until you close. Lock your rate when the timing makes sense, and read your Closing Disclosure carefully the moment you receive it, comparing it line by line with your Loan Estimate. Do a final walk-through to confirm agreed repairs were done and nothing has changed. Then set up utilities: Evergy (electric), WaterOne (water), Kansas Gas Service (gas) and Johnson County Wastewater (sewer). Trash is usually a private hauler or HOA-arranged. Frequently asked questions

How much should I budget for property taxes on a 66223 home?

Kansas assesses residential property at 11.5% of appraised value, and the tax equals assessed value times the total mill levy divided by 1,000. Total levies depend on the exact taxing districts, so use the property tax estimator and verify with Johnson County.

Is a radon test really necessary?

It is the only way to know a home's radon level, and it is inexpensive relative to the purchase. Include it in your inspection period so you can negotiate mitigation if needed.

Can I rely on the listing for the school assignment?

No. Confirm the assigned schools for the specific address directly with Blue Valley USD 229 before your contingencies expire.

How long does closing usually take?

For a financed purchase, roughly 30 to 60 days from accepted offer is a typical range. Cash purchases can close faster.

Should I waive the inspection to win a bidding war?

Waiving it removes your main protection on the costliest issues, such as foundation and roof. A shorter inspection window is usually a safer way to compete.
