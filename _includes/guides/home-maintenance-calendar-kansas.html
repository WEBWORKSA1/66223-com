Key takeaways:
  • Do the big jobs before each season starts: storm prep in late winter, HVAC cooling checks in spring, freeze prep in fall.
  • Test your sump pump and clean your gutters before the spring storm season, not during it.
  • Most lawns in the Kansas City area are cool-season grasses, so fall is when they need the most work, not spring.
  • Keep a dated photo log of your home's condition. If a storm ever hits, it's your best evidence.

Why a Kansas home needs its own calendar

A home in 66223 deals with a wide swing of conditions in one year. Winter brings hard freezes that can split exposed pipes and hoses. Spring and early summer bring severe thunderstorms, hail and the occasional tornado warning. Summer heat and humidity keep your air conditioner running for months. A generic national checklist misses that timing. The calendar below follows the Kansas year, and most tasks take an afternoon or less.

Treat the months as targets, not deadlines. Weather moves a little every year, so watch the forecast from the National Weather Service and shift a task a few weeks earlier or later when you need to.

Spring (March to May)

This is the busiest stretch of the year and also the riskiest, since severe weather picks up. Front-load the water and roof jobs.

Spring checklist

  • Test the sump pump. Slowly pour a bucket of water into the pit until the float rises. The pump should kick on, empty the pit and shut off. If it hums without pumping, cycles nonstop or doesn't start, get it repaired now.
  • Consider a battery backup for the sump pump. Storms that bring heavy rain also knock out power, and a pump with no power can't protect your basement.
  • Clean the gutters and downspouts. Make sure downspouts carry water several feet away from the foundation. A common rule of thumb is at least 4 to 6 feet.
  • Walk the roof line from the ground. With binoculars, look for missing, curled or cracked shingles and loose flashing around chimneys and vents.
  • Schedule an A/C tune-up before the first hot week, when HVAC companies get booked up.
  • Replace the HVAC filter and clear leaves and debris from around the outdoor condenser unit.
  • Turn outdoor water back on only after the risk of a hard freeze has passed. Check each hose bib for leaks inside the wall the first time you run it.
  • Restock a storm kit: flashlights, batteries, a weather radio, phone chargers, water and any medications.
  • Pick a shelter spot in the lowest interior room of the house, away from windows, and make sure everyone in the house knows where it is.
Storm rule: after any hailstorm, take photos of your roof, siding, gutters, windows and car before you clean up or call anyone. Then read the policy before you sign anything from a contractor who knocks on your door.

Fall (September to November)

Fall is the second big season: you get the lawn ready for its best growing window and the house ready for freezing weather.

Lawn: cool-season grass care

Many Kansas City-area lawns are cool-season grasses such as tall fescue and Kentucky bluegrass. They grow best in cool weather, so early fall is the main window to:

  • Core aerate compacted soil, especially clay-heavy yards.
  • Overseed thin or bare areas so new grass gets established before winter.
  • Fertilize following the label. Many homeowners make fall the main feeding of the year.
  • Keep mowing until the grass stops growing, and mulch or remove leaves so they don't smother the turf.

Check your HOA rules before big yard projects. Many south Overland Park neighborhoods have standards for lawns, fences and exterior changes.

Freeze prep checklist

  • Disconnect, drain and store garden hoses before the first hard freeze.
  • Shut off and drain outdoor faucets if they have an interior shutoff valve. Add insulated faucet covers.
  • Have your lawn irrigation system blown out and winterized.
  • Schedule a furnace inspection and replace the filter.
  • Clean the gutters again once most leaves have fallen.
  • Seal gaps around windows, doors and where pipes enter the house.
  • Test the sump pump one more time before winter rain and snowmelt.
  • Check the fireplace and chimney before first use if you burn wood.

Year-round habits that pay off

  • Keep a maintenance log. Note the date, the task and any receipts. It helps with warranty claims, insurance and resale. Buyers like seeing documented care, as our guide to selling in 66223 explains.
  • Know your shutoffs: water main, gas meter and electrical panel.
  • Set aside a repair fund. A common rule of thumb is to budget roughly 1% to 2% of your home's value per year for upkeep, but treat that only as a starting point. Older homes and homes with original roofs or HVAC need more.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I test my sump pump?

At least twice a year, in early spring before storm season and again in fall. Also test it after any long power outage, and listen for it during heavy rain to confirm it's cycling normally.

When should I overseed my lawn in the Kansas City area?

For cool-season grasses like tall fescue and Kentucky bluegrass, early fall is generally the best window, since soil is still warm and air temperatures are cooling. Spring seeding works too, but young grass has less time to establish before summer heat.

What's the most important freeze-prep step?

Disconnecting garden hoses and protecting outdoor faucets. A hose left attached can trap water in the faucet, which can freeze and crack the pipe inside the wall, and you may not notice the leak until spring.

How often should I change my HVAC filter?

Check it monthly and replace it when it looks dirty. Depending on the filter type, pets and household size, that's often every one to three months, and more often during peak heating and cooling seasons. Follow the filter manufacturer's guidance.
