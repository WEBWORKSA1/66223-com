Pricing strategy

Pricing is the single biggest lever you control. Overpricing tends to backfire: the listing sits, buyers wonder what is wrong, and price cuts often land you below where a correct initial price would have.

  • Use true comparables: recent closed sales of similar size, age, condition, lot and subdivision. Active listings tell you about competition; closed sales tell you about value.
  • Adjust honestly: a finished basement, updated kitchen or newer roof adds value; deferred maintenance subtracts it, often by more than the repair cost.
  • Price at search thresholds: buyers filter by round numbers. A home listed just above a common cutoff can disappear from many searches.
  • Plan your first two weeks: agree with your agent in advance what showing and offer activity would justify holding firm or adjusting.

Prep and staging

Focus spending where buyers notice and where inspectors look. A rough priority order that works for most homes:

  1. Repair: leaks, broken fixtures, damaged siding, cracked windows, failing caulk and anything a buyer's inspector will write up.
  2. Clean and declutter: deep clean, remove a third of the furniture and most personal items, and clear closets and the garage.
  3. Refresh: neutral paint, updated light bulbs, new cabinet hardware where it is dated.
  4. Curb appeal: mowed lawn, edged beds, fresh mulch, a clean front door and working exterior lights.
  5. Stage key rooms: living room, kitchen and primary bedroom matter most in photos.

Skip large remodels right before selling. Buyers rarely pay back the full cost of a major project, and their taste may differ from yours.

Consider a pre-listing inspection

A pre-listing inspection costs a few hundred dollars as a typical range and can save you far more at the negotiating table. It surfaces problems before a buyer's inspector does, so you can repair, get quotes, or price with the issue in mind.

In Kansas, pay attention to three areas buyers focus on: radon (consider a test and, if needed, a mitigation system), the foundation and drainage, and the roof. Spring and early-summer storms, including hail, are a real factor here, so have documentation ready for roof age, repairs and any insurance claims. The hail and roof insurance guide explains what to gather.

Disclosure: Once you know about a material defect, you generally need to disclose it. Ask your agent or a real estate attorney exactly what Kansas disclosure forms require of you.

Seasonality and timing

In much of the Kansas City metro, spring and early summer tend to bring the most buyer activity, especially from families who want to move between school years. That also means more competing listings. Fall can still work well with fewer competitors, and winter listings can attract serious, motivated buyers. As a rule of thumb, start prep six to eight weeks before your target list date so photos are taken with the house ready and the yard looking its best. Your personal timing, such as where you are moving next, usually matters more than squeezing out the perfect week.

Choosing a listing agent

Interview at least three agents. Ask each the same questions so answers are comparable.

Questions to ask

  • What comparables did you use for my price, and why did you exclude others?
  • How many homes have you sold in 66223 and nearby ZIPs in the last year, and how did list price compare with sale price?
  • What exactly is in your marketing plan: photography, video, floor plans, online placement, open houses?
  • What is your commission, what does it cover, and how is buyer-agent compensation being handled?
  • How long is the listing agreement, and can I cancel if I am unhappy with the service?
  • Who will I actually communicate with day to day, and how quickly do you respond?

Be cautious of an agent who simply quotes the highest price. Ask to see the evidence. If you want introductions to vetted local agents, use free matching.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to list in 66223?

Spring and early summer typically bring the most buyers, but also more competing listings. The best time is usually when your home is fully prepared and your next move is lined up.

Is a pre-listing inspection worth it?

For most sellers, yes. It lets you fix or price in issues before a buyer uses them to renegotiate under a tight deadline.

Should I renovate before selling?

Usually not beyond repairs, paint, cleaning and curb appeal. Major remodels rarely return their full cost at sale.

How do I estimate what I will net?

Start with a realistic sale price, then subtract your loan payoff, commissions, title costs, concessions, repairs and prorated taxes. Ask your agent and title company for a written seller net sheet.

Can I negotiate the commission?

Commissions are negotiable. Compare what each agent includes for the fee rather than choosing on price alone.
