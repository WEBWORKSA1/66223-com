Key takeaways:
  • Your real monthly payment is PITI: principal, interest, taxes and insurance, plus PMI or HOA dues if they apply.
  • Conventional, FHA, VA and USDA loans each fit different buyers; eligibility and terms are set by the lender and program rules.
  • The 28/36 debt-to-income guideline is a rule of thumb for comfort, not a lending law.
  • Get Loan Estimates from at least three lenders on the same day and compare the APR, fees and points side by side.

The main loan types

Conventional loans

Conventional loans are not insured by a government agency. They generally suit borrowers with solid credit and stable income. Down payments can be lower than 20%, but below 20% you will usually pay private mortgage insurance (PMI). Conventional loans offer the most flexibility in property types and terms.

FHA loans

FHA loans are insured by the Federal Housing Administration. They are often easier to qualify for with lower credit scores or smaller down payments. The trade-off is mortgage insurance premiums, which can include an upfront charge plus a monthly premium and may last longer than PMI on a conventional loan. Check current program rules with your lender.

VA loans

VA loans are guaranteed by the Department of Veterans Affairs for eligible service members, veterans and some surviving spouses. They often allow no down payment and do not carry monthly mortgage insurance, though a funding fee usually applies unless you qualify for an exemption. You will need a Certificate of Eligibility.

USDA loans

USDA loans are for eligible rural and some suburban areas and have household income limits. Much of suburban Overland Park is unlikely to qualify, but eligibility is set by location and changes over time, so check the specific address with a lender rather than assuming either way.

PITI: what you actually pay each month

  • Principal: the part that pays down your balance and builds equity.
  • Interest: the lender's charge for the loan. Early in a 30-year loan, most of each payment is interest.
  • Taxes: property taxes, usually collected monthly into escrow.
  • Insurance: homeowners insurance, also usually escrowed.

Add PMI or FHA mortgage insurance and any HOA dues to get your true housing cost. The mortgage calculator breaks out each piece.

PMI and how to get rid of it

PMI protects the lender, not you, when you put less than 20% down on a conventional loan. It is not permanent. On many conventional loans you can ask to remove it once your balance reaches 80% of the original value, and it typically ends automatically at a lower threshold. Rising home values can help, sometimes with a new appraisal. Ask your lender exactly how removal works on your loan before you sign. FHA mortgage insurance follows different rules.

Points: paying now to save later

Discount points are upfront fees that lower your interest rate. As a rule of thumb, one point costs 1% of the loan amount. To decide whether points make sense, divide the cost of the points by the monthly savings to find how many months it takes to break even. If you expect to sell or refinance before that, points usually are not worth it. Watch for lender credits too, which work the opposite way: a higher rate in exchange for lower closing costs.

Debt-to-income: rules of thumb

Lenders look at your debt-to-income ratio (DTI), which is monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income.

Rule of thumb (28/36): Keep total housing costs at or below about 28% of gross monthly income, and all debt payments, including housing, at or below about 36%. Many loan programs allow higher ratios, but higher is not the same as comfortable.

How to shop for a rate

  1. Check your credit first. Fix errors and avoid new debt before applying.
  2. Apply with at least three lenders within a short window, such as a bank, a credit union and a mortgage broker. Multiple mortgage inquiries in a short period are generally treated as one for credit scoring, but confirm with your lender.
  3. Compare Loan Estimates line by line. Look at the rate, APR, points, lender fees and cash to close. A low rate with high fees can cost more than a slightly higher rate with low fees.
  4. Ask about rate locks: how long the lock lasts, what an extension costs, and whether there is a float-down option.
  5. Negotiate. Show a competing Loan Estimate and ask a lender to match or beat it.

Escrow and Kansas property taxes

Most lenders collect property taxes and insurance through an escrow account. Each month a portion of your payment goes into escrow, and the servicer pays the bills when due. At closing, expect to fund an initial escrow cushion.

Kansas assesses residential property at 11.5% of appraised market value. The tax equals assessed value times the total mill levy divided by 1,000. Homes also get an exemption on the first $75,000 of appraised value for the statewide 20-mill school levy, a saving of about $172.50 a year. In Overland Park, the city's levy is about 14.5 mills for 2026, roughly 13% of a typical total bill; county, school, state and other districts make up the rest.

  • Escrow analysis: your servicer reviews the account periodically. If your appraised value or levy rises, your payment can go up even on a fixed-rate loan.
  • Valuation notices: the Johnson County Appraiser's Office sets values, and appeals are possible after valuation notices go out. Verify current timelines with Johnson County.
  • Estimate before you buy: use the property tax estimator and read the Johnson County property tax guide.
Watch out: A seller's current tax bill may reflect an older valuation or exemptions that will not carry over to you. Base your budget on a realistic estimate at your purchase price.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need 20% down to buy a home in Kansas?

No. Many conventional, FHA and VA options allow less. Below 20% on a conventional loan, expect PMI.

Is the 28/36 rule a lending requirement?

No. It is a rule of thumb for affordability. Lenders set their own limits by program, and many allow higher ratios.

Can I get a USDA loan in Overland Park?

Most of the city is unlikely to qualify, but eligibility depends on the specific address and income limits. Ask a lender to check.

Why did my payment go up on a fixed-rate loan?

Your principal and interest stay fixed, but escrowed taxes and insurance can rise. An escrow analysis then adjusts your monthly payment.

Are points worth paying?

Only if you keep the loan past the break-even point, which is the cost of the points divided by the monthly savings.
