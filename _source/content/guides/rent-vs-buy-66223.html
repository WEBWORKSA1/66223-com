  • In 66223 the median gross rent is $1,718 a month and the median home value was $437,500 in 2024, so owning usually costs more per month at first.
  • Buying tends to win only if you stay long enough to recover transaction costs; a common rule of thumb is five years or more.
  • Count the hidden costs of owning: property taxes, insurance, maintenance, HOA dues and the return your down payment could earn elsewhere.
  • Renting wins when your timeline is short, your job is uncertain, or buying would drain your emergency savings.

What owning really costs each month

Compare rent with the full cost of owning, not just the mortgage payment.

  • Principal and interest: depends on price, down payment and rate. Run it in the mortgage calculator.
  • Property taxes: Kansas assesses homes at 11.5% of appraised value, and tax equals assessed value times total mills divided by 1,000. Overland Park's city levy is about 14.5 mills for 2026, roughly 13% of a typical total bill; the county, schools, state and other districts make up the rest. Estimate yours with the property tax estimator.
  • Homeowners insurance: hail and spring storms are a real factor in Kansas, which can affect premiums and deductibles. Get actual quotes.
  • PMI: usually applies to conventional loans with less than 20% down.
  • HOA dues: common in 66223 subdivisions and highly variable.
  • Maintenance and repairs: a widely used rule of thumb is to budget 1% to 2% of the home's value per year. Roofs, HVAC, water heaters and driveways eventually need replacing.
  • Utilities and yard care: often higher in a detached house than in an apartment.

The costs people forget

Transaction costs

Buying involves closing costs, and selling later involves agent commissions, title fees and prep. Combined, buying and selling can easily consume several percent of the home's value as a typical range. Those costs are why short stays usually favor renting.

Opportunity cost of the down payment

Money you put into a down payment can no longer be invested elsewhere. A fair comparison assumes the renter invests the difference in down payment and monthly cost, and lets that grow.

Your time and risk

Owners carry repair surprises and market swings. Renters carry rent increases and the possibility of a non-renewal. Neither is risk-free.

Break-even logic, step by step

The break-even point is the number of years after which owning comes out ahead of renting. Here is how to reason through it:

  1. Add up the cost of owning each year: interest (not principal, which you keep as equity), property taxes, insurance, PMI, HOA dues, maintenance, and lost investment return on your down payment.
  2. Add up the cost of renting each year: rent, renter's insurance and expected annual rent increases.
  3. Account for one-time costs: closing costs now, and selling costs when you leave.
  4. Add appreciation and equity: owners gain from principal paydown and any rise in home value. Do not assume the past pace continues; test a low-growth and a flat scenario.
  5. Find the crossover year: the year when cumulative owning costs, net of equity built, drop below cumulative renting costs.

The rent vs. buy calculator does this math for you. Change one assumption at a time, such as appreciation or how long you stay, to see which inputs actually move the answer.

Rule of thumb: If the calculator's break-even lands well beyond how long you are confident you will stay, rent. If it lands well inside it, buying deserves a serious look.

When renting wins

  • You expect to move within a few years for work or family.
  • Your income is new, variable or uncertain.
  • Buying would leave you without an emergency fund after closing.
  • You are new to the area and want to learn neighborhoods, commutes and school boundaries first. The moving to Overland Park guide helps here.
  • Your credit or debt-to-income ratio would push you into a high rate. Renting while you improve both can save real money.

When buying wins

  • You plan to stay well past your break-even year.
  • You have the down payment plus a healthy reserve for repairs.
  • Your total monthly owning cost is comfortable, not a stretch.
  • You value stability, control over the property, and building equity.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to rent or buy in 66223?

On a month-one basis, renting is usually cheaper given a median rent of $1,718 against a median home value of $437,500. Over a long enough stay, buying can win once equity and appreciation are counted.

How long do I need to stay for buying to make sense?

A common rule of thumb is at least five years, but your break-even depends on price, rate, down payment and appreciation. Use the rent vs. buy calculator for your numbers.

Should I count principal payments as a cost?

No. Principal builds equity you keep. Interest, taxes, insurance, PMI, HOA dues and maintenance are the true costs.

Will home values keep rising like they have?

No one can promise that. Values rose from $263,300 in 2011 to $437,500 in 2024, but test flat and slow-growth scenarios before you commit.
