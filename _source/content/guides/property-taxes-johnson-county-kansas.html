Key takeaways:
  • Kansas assesses homes at 11.5% of appraised market value. Tax = assessed value x total mills / 1,000.
  • Your total mill levy combines the city, county, schools, state and other districts, and it varies by address.
  • Homes get an exemption on the first $75,000 of appraised value for the 20-mill state school levy, worth about $172.50 a year.
  • You can appeal your valuation after you receive your notice. The notice itself tells you how and by when.

The formula in plain English

Kansas property tax for a home works in three steps:

  1. Appraised value. The Johnson County Appraiser's Office estimates your home's market value.
  2. Assessed value. Residential property is assessed at 11.5% of that appraised value.
  3. Tax. Multiply the assessed value by your total mill levy and divide by 1,000. One mill is $1 of tax for every $1,000 of assessed value.

That is it. Everything else, including exemptions and appeals, is about getting one of those inputs right.

Who levies what

Your bill is not one tax; it is a stack of levies from different taxing bodies. For a home in Overland Park, the pieces include:

  • City of Overland Park: about 14.5 mills for 2026, the lowest of all Johnson County cities. The average homeowner's city portion is about $880 for 2026, roughly 13% of a total bill.
  • Johnson County government: about 24.1 mills for FY2025, the lowest county taxing-district rate among Kansas's 105 counties. The county has cut its levy seven times in eight years.
  • School district: Blue Valley USD 229 serves ZIP 66223, and school levies make up a large share of most bills.
  • State of Kansas: including the statewide 20-mill school levy.
  • Other districts: such as community college, library, parks, fire or drainage districts, depending on your location.

Because the combination depends on where your property sits, two homes with the same value in different parts of the county can owe different amounts. Your tax statement lists every levy that applies to your parcel.

The $75,000 school-levy exemption

Kansas levies a statewide 20-mill property tax to fund schools. Residential homes get an exemption on the first $75,000 of appraised value for that levy only. The math: $75,000 x 11.5% = $8,625 of assessed value, and $8,625 x 20 / 1,000 = $172.50 a year.

Two things to understand:

  • The exemption applies only to the 20-mill state school levy, not to your whole bill.
  • The saving is a fixed amount of about $172.50, so it matters more proportionally on lower-value homes.

Appealing your valuation

You cannot appeal the mill levy itself through the appraiser, but you can challenge the appraised value. The Johnson County Appraiser's Office handles valuations, and appeals are possible after valuation notices are sent. Here is how to approach it:

  1. Read your valuation notice the day it arrives. It states the appraised value, how to appeal and the deadline. Put the deadline on your calendar immediately.
  2. Check the property facts. Square footage, bedroom and bathroom counts, finished basement area, lot size and condition errors are the easiest wins.
  3. Gather comparable sales. Look for recent sales of similar homes nearby. A local real estate agent can often pull these for you.
  4. Document condition issues. Photos and repair estimates for problems like foundation work, roof damage or outdated systems support a lower value.
  5. Stay factual. Appeals succeed on evidence about market value, not on the size of the bill or a tax-rate complaint.

Follow the exact process and deadlines listed on your notice and on the Johnson County website. Procedures can change, so do not rely on what a neighbor did in a prior year.

Budgeting and escrow tips

  • Do not budget from the seller's bill. A recent purchase price can lead to a new appraised value. Estimate taxes on your expected value, not the previous owner's.
  • Expect escrow adjustments. If your lender collects taxes in escrow, your monthly payment can rise after an annual escrow analysis when taxes go up. Ask your lender how they handle shortages.
  • Set aside monthly if you pay directly. Divide your estimated annual bill by 12 and move that amount to a separate savings account each month.
  • Review every year. Compare each new valuation notice to recent sales. Catching an error early is easier than fixing it later.
  • Factor taxes into affordability. In the example above, property tax adds about $468 a month. Run your full payment through the mortgage calculator and read mortgage basics for Kansas homebuyers.

Frequently asked questions

What is the residential assessment rate in Kansas?

Kansas assesses residential property at 11.5% of appraised market value.

How do I calculate my Johnson County property tax?

Multiply your appraised value by 11.5% to get assessed value, multiply by your total mill levy, and divide by 1,000. Then subtract about $172.50 for the school-levy exemption if it applies.

What is Overland Park's city mill levy?

About 14.5 mills for 2026, the lowest of all Johnson County cities. The city's share is roughly 13% of a typical total bill.

Can I appeal my property taxes?

You can appeal your appraised value with the Johnson County Appraiser's Office after you receive your valuation notice. The notice explains the process and deadline.

Where do I find my actual mill levy?

Your annual tax statement lists every levy that applies to your property. Johnson County's website also provides tax and levy information.
