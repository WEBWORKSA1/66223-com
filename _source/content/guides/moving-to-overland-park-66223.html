Why people move to 66223

ZIP 66223 sits in the southern part of Overland Park in Johnson County, Kansas, on the Kansas side of the Kansas City metro. About 24,843 people lived here at the 2020 Census, in 9,836 households, which works out to roughly 2.53 people per home. That household size tells you a lot: this is family territory, with a median age of 38.4.

The reasons people give for choosing it tend to cluster around a few themes:

  • Schools. The ZIP is served by Blue Valley USD 229, and many buyers arrive with school boundaries already in mind.
  • City reputation. Overland Park ranked #10 on Niche's Best Cities to Live in America list for 2026, #2 on WalletHub's 2026 list of best places to raise a family and #6 on Livability.com's 2025 Best Places to Live in the U.S.
  • Relatively restrained local tax rates. Overland Park's city mill levy, about 14.5 mills for 2026, is the lowest of any Johnson County city, and the county's own levy is the lowest county rate in Kansas.
  • Parks and green space. The city has more than 80 parks, plus the Overland Park Arboretum & Botanical Gardens, Deanna Rose Children's Farmstead and an award-winning farmers' market.

The trade-off is cost. BestPlaces puts the overall cost of living about 6.2% above the US average, and home values have climbed sharply: the median rose from $263,300 in 2011 to $437,500 in 2024.

What the neighborhoods feel like

Expect a suburban layout: subdivisions with curving streets, cul-de-sacs, HOA-managed common areas and commercial corridors you drive to rather than walk to. Around 56% of homes are owner-occupied and 44% are rented, so you will find a real rental market alongside single-family neighborhoods, not just one or the other.

When you tour, judge each neighborhood on the things that will shape your daily life:

  • HOA rules and dues. Ask for the covenants before you commit. They can govern fences, sheds, exterior colors, parking and even trash pickup.
  • School assignment. Boundaries do not always follow subdivision lines. Verify the address with Blue Valley USD 229.
  • Traffic at peak hours. A quiet street at 2 p.m. can sit next to a backed-up arterial at 7:45 a.m.
  • Construction. Newer areas may still have active building nearby. Ask what is planned on vacant lots.

Commuting by car in the KC metro

With a Walk Score of 33, 66223 is car-dependent. Groceries, school drop-off, the gym and most errands will involve driving. Plan on it and you will not be disappointed; plan against it and you will be.

A practical approach to commute planning:

  1. Do a test drive at rush hour. Before you sign anything, drive from the candidate address to your workplace on a weekday morning and back in the evening. Map apps give estimates; your own trip gives reality.
  2. Count the household's cars, not just yours. Two working adults and a teen driver can mean three vehicles, three insurance policies and three sets of registration fees.
  3. Price in fuel or charging, maintenance and tires. These add up faster in a ZIP where nearly every trip is by car.
  4. Ask about hybrid schedules. If you work from home part of the week, a longer commute may be perfectly livable. See our remote work guide for setting up a home office here.

Your first 90 days

Days 1 to 30: get the basics running

  • Transfer or open accounts with Evergy (electric), WaterOne (water), Kansas Gas Service (natural gas) and Johnson County Wastewater (sewer).
  • Confirm trash service. It is typically handled by private haulers or arranged through your HOA.
  • Set up internet and test coverage for your cell carrier inside the house.
  • Enroll children in school as early as possible. Blue Valley USD 229 lists current enrollment steps and required documents.
  • Find your nearest urgent care, pharmacy and hospital before you need them.

Days 31 to 60: make it official

  • Get your Kansas driver's license and register your vehicles. Check the Kansas Department of Revenue and Johnson County for current requirements and deadlines.
  • Register to vote at your new address.
  • Update your address with your bank, employer, insurer and any subscriptions.
  • Review your homeowner's or renter's insurance for wind and hail coverage.

Days 61 to 90: settle in

  • Build a severe weather plan: know where you will shelter, and set up weather alerts on every phone in the house.
  • Explore the parks, the Arboretum and the farmers' market, and sign up for city updates from the City of Overland Park.
  • Meet your neighbors and join the HOA or neighborhood group if there is one.
  • Line up a few trusted service pros before something breaks.

Is 66223 the right fit?

It fits well if you want suburban space, are comfortable driving for nearly everything and place a high value on schools and parks. It fits less well if you want to walk to coffee, live car-free or keep housing costs near the national average. Be honest about which group you are in before you start touring.

Frequently asked questions

What city is ZIP 66223 in?

66223 is in Overland Park, in Johnson County, Kansas, part of the Kansas City metro area. It is in the Central time zone and uses area code 913.

Can I live in 66223 without a car?

It would be difficult. The ZIP has a Walk Score of 33, which rates it as car-dependent. Most households here should plan on at least one vehicle per working adult.

Which school district serves 66223?

Blue Valley USD 229 serves the ZIP. Specific school assignments depend on the exact address, so confirm with the district before you buy or sign a lease.

Is it cheaper to rent or buy in 66223?

It depends on how long you plan to stay, your down payment and current mortgage rates. The median gross rent is $1,718 a month, while the median home value was $437,500 in 2024. Compare your own scenario with the rent vs. buy calculator.

When is severe weather most likely?

Thunderstorms, hail and tornadoes are most common in spring and early summer in Kansas. Check forecasts and alerts at the National Weather Service and have a shelter plan in place before the season starts.
