What the numbers mean

It's an affluent, educated, owner-leaning ZIP — but not all owners. Two-thirds of adults hold at least a bachelor's degree and household income runs roughly 50% above the national median, yet about 44% of homes are rented. That mix supports both a strong resale market and steady rental demand.

Home values have climbed fast. The median value rose from about $263,300 in 2011 to about $437,500 in 2024. For buyers, that means affordability depends heavily on rates and taxes — run your own scenario in the mortgage calculator. For owners, it means it's worth checking your valuation each year; see how Johnson County property taxes work.

Plan on driving. A Walk Score of 33 means most errands need a car. Factor two vehicles into your budget if you have a two-commuter household.

Taxes: low city levy, full bill still meaningful. Overland Park's city levy is the lowest in Johnson County, but the city is only around 13% of a property tax bill. Schools, the county and the state make up the rest, which is why the total bill is what you should budget for.
